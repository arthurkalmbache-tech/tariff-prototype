Chapter 22
Chapter 22 is the customs chapter every bottle of wine on earth travels under: beverages, spirits and vinegar.
Put my catalogue on watch

Chapter 22 is a watch service for wine exporters. You give it your bottle list once. It then reads every tariff, duty and trade measure published in the markets you ship to, tests each one against your own lines, and tells you which of your bottles it names and what it costs you. It does not forecast, and it does not email you about things that do not touch your book.

What follows is a live demonstration rather than a brochure. It walks one real measure through the three questions Chapter 22 answers — what it names, whether measures of its kind actually arrive, and what it costs — using the book of one illustrative exporter: eleven wines, his live orders in each market, and real published measures as published. Change the market in the bar above and the whole page re-reads for that market. Picture your own list in place of his.

01

First question: does the wording name your bottles?

Trade measures are never written in grape varieties. They are written in customs codes and country of origin, which is why reading the news tells you a measure exists but not whether it reaches you. So the first thing Chapter 22 does is mechanical: it takes the measure exactly as published and tests it against your list, one bottle at a time.

02

Second question: does this kind of thing actually arrive?

Knowing a measure names your bottles is not the same as knowing it will ever bite. Some announcements become law in weeks; some are traded away in the negotiation they were invented for. Chapter 22 will not tell you which this one is, because nobody honestly can. What it will do is show you every comparable measure in this market and how far each one actually got, so you can judge for yourself.

How to read the table below

One row, one measureEvery entry is a real measure aimed at this category in this market, drawn from the public record, oldest at the top.
The five dotsA trade measure passes through five stages before it binds anybody: floated in talks, announced publicly, published as a legal instrument, in force, then settled. The dots are those stages.

03

Third question: at what point does the business stop working?

"A tariff" is four different things depending on where you ship, and each bites in a different place: a percentage of your invoice, a charge on the alcohol in the bottle, a duty you can escape by raising your price, or a shelf you are simply removed from. Chapter 22 models the one that actually applies in the market you have chosen, against this exporter's own open orders, and finds the level at which the money runs out.

How to use the model below

The cost chainEvery hand between the cellar and the shelf. It is what decides how much of a duty you can pass on, and how much you have to absorb yourself.
The sliderSets the level of the measure. One drag recalculates the figures above it, the chart, and every order row at once.
The chartA single line, drawn across every level that measure could take. The marked point on it is the level at which the money runs out — the one number this page exists to produce.
The order rowsThis exporter's own open orders in the market you have chosen: what he paid, what he invoices, what the bottle retails for, and what survives at the level you have set.
Or skip the draggingPress Find the line where it stops working and the model walks to the breaking point by itself.

04

Then you decide — and here is what Chapter 22 will never do for you

You now have the three facts: which of your lines the wording names, how far measures of this kind have actually got in this market, and the rate at which the arithmetic stops working. The decision after that is yours, and it should be. A tool that pretended otherwise would be worse than no tool, so here is the boundary, stated plainly and before you are asked for anything.

It won't predictNo likelihood scores, no confidence percentages. Whether a measure lands is a political judgement, and dressing a guess in a number would only make you trust it more than you should.
It won't classify or adviseYour codes come from you or your forwarder. Chapter 22 reads measures against them; it is not a customs broker and it does not give legal advice.
It won't speak to your buyersThe conversation with your importer is the whole relationship. You have that conversation. Chapter 22 makes sure you walk into it already knowing the arithmetic.
It won't touch your accountsCosts and prices are typed in once, by you, and stay in your own workspace. No ledger connection, no bank access, nothing to breach.

I built this after an exporter described his March 2025 to me: weeks spent reading coverage of a 200% tariff that was never published, while the 15% that did arrive reached him through his importer. He was not short of a forecast. He was short of three answers — which of his own lines the wording named, whether measures of that kind had ever actually arrived in that market, and the rate at which his price stops working. Those three things are all this page does. If they are not what costs you your weeks, tell me that and I will stop building it.

05

Put your catalogue on watch

Setting it up is the catalogue you just saw, except yours: your bottle list with a customs code and origin against each line, your open orders, and the markets you ship to. That is the whole of the work, and it is done once. From then on you hear from Chapter 22 only when something is published that names one of your own lines — and when it does, you get the three answers above rather than a link to an article.

No price is quoted here because none is fixed yet. Ask, and you get a straight answer for a book your size — along with what it would be worth to you, which is the part I am still working out.

  1. You send your catalogue once. A spreadsheet is fine. Line, customs code, origin, strength, your cost. Nothing else.
  2. Chapter 22 watches the official sources, not the news. Published notices and official journals in every market you ship to.
  3. When a measure names one of your lines, you get one page. Which lines, where the measure sits against the ones before it, and the level at which your margin reaches zero.

Silence the rest of the time. If a month goes by with nothing written that touches your bottles, you hear nothing — and that is the service working.
